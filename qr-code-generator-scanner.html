How to Use the QR Code Generator & Scanner

  1. Choose a Mode: Switch between Generate (to create a QR code) or Scan (to decode one).
  2. To Generate: Pick a QR type — URL, phone, Wi-Fi, vCard, WhatsApp, and more — enter the details, and click Generate.
  3. To Scan: Upload an image containing a QR code and it decodes instantly.
  4. Download or Copy: Save your generated QR as PNG/SVG/PDF, or copy a scanned code's decoded content.

Why Use This Tool

Creating and reading QR codes are two sides of the same everyday need — a small business generates a WhatsApp QR code for their storefront, while a customer scans a code from a screenshot to check where it leads before opening it on their phone. Instead of bookmarking a separate QR generator and a separate QR scanner, this tool combines both free, instant, and 100% private in your browser, with no app download and no sign-up required.

How It Works

The Generate mode uses a JavaScript QR encoding library to convert your entered text, link, or contact details into a scannable matrix pattern rendered directly on the page. The Scan mode loads a JavaScript QR decoding library that reads an uploaded image's pixel data to detect and decode the matrix pattern back into text. Both run entirely in your browser — nothing you generate or scan is ever uploaded to a server.

Key Features

Nine QR types for generation — URL/Text, Phone, Email, SMS, Wi-Fi, vCard contact cards, Location, Calendar Event, and WhatsApp Direct — each formatted automatically so the scanning app opens the correct action. Adjustable size, foreground/background color, error correction level, and an optional center logo. Download as PNG, SVG, or PDF, plus a batch generator that creates many QR codes at once as a ZIP file. The Scan mode detects and labels the content type (link, email, phone, Wi-Fi, contact card, or plain text), shows a live image preview, and lets you download the decoded result as text.

Common Use Cases

A small business generates a WhatsApp Direct QR code for their shop counter or product packaging. A marketer creates a Wi-Fi QR code for guests at an event. A print shop verifies a client-supplied QR code image actually decodes to the correct URL before running a large print job. A security-conscious user decodes a QR code from an image first to inspect where it leads before scanning it live with their phone camera. An event organizer batch-generates dozens of unique QR codes at once for tickets or badges.

Tips for Best Results

When generating, choose a higher error correction level if you plan to add a center logo, since it adds redundancy that keeps the code scannable. When scanning, use a clear, well-lit, high-resolution image — a photo cropped tightly around just the QR code decodes far more reliably than a full-page photo. For WhatsApp QR codes, double-check the country code in the phone number before printing.

Privacy & Security

Because ToolNest processes everything entirely within your browser using client-side JavaScript, your generated codes, uploaded images, and decoded content never leave your device. There is no server-side storage, no third-party analytics attached to your data, and no risk of a breach exposing something you generated or scanned months ago.
