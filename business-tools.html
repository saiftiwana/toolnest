Ten Tools for Freelancers, Small Businesses and Job Seekers

Running a small business or looking for work means producing a steady stream of documents that all have to look professional but none of which justify a monthly software subscription. This category covers ten of them. The Invoice Generator produces a numbered, itemised invoice with tax lines and payment details, ready to export as a PDF. The Resume / CV Builder assembles a clean, one or two page document in a layout that automated applicant tracking systems can read. The Instant Contract / Agreement Draft Generator produces a starting draft for common arrangements, and the Client Proposal & Scope Generator turns a brief into a structured proposal with deliverables, timeline and pricing. The QR Code Generator & Scanner and Barcode Generator handle product labels, menus and payment links. The SaaS Runway & Cash Burn Rate Calculator shows how many months of operation your cash reserve covers. The Rishta Biodata Studio and Wedding & Shaadi Budget Planner serve South Asian family planning needs specifically.

Where to Start

For freelancers, the Invoice Generator is usually the first tool worth learning properly: set your business details and tax rate once, then reuse the same layout every month, keeping invoice numbers sequential because that is what accountants and tax authorities expect. Before the invoice comes the agreement, so use the Client Proposal & Scope Generator to write down deliverables and revision limits, then the Contract Generator for a signable draft. Job seekers should treat the Resume / CV Builder as a formatting tool rather than a writing tool: the content still needs to be tailored to each posting, but the builder ensures the file parses correctly when an employer's software reads it. Shopkeepers and restaurants can generate a QR code that points to a menu or a payment link in seconds, and the Barcode Generator produces scannable CODE128 labels for stock. Founders should run the Runway Calculator before, not after, a hiring decision.

Why These Documents Should Never Touch a Server

An invoice contains your client's name and address, your bank details and your revenue. A resume contains your home address, phone number, employment history and often your date of birth. A contract contains both parties' legal identities and commercial terms. Every one of these is exactly what identity theft and business email compromise depend on, and uploading them to a free web service means trusting an unknown party with all of it. ToolNest builds these documents on your device: the form you fill in stays in browser memory, the PDF is rendered locally, and the finished file is delivered as a normal download. No account is created, no email address is collected, no copy is retained, and there is no database anywhere holding your client list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my invoice or resume data uploaded?

No. Everything is entered, laid out and exported inside your browser. No client details, bank information or employment history is sent to a server or stored by ToolNest.

Are the generated contracts legally binding?

They are drafting starting points, not legal advice. Laws differ by country and situation, so have any agreement that carries real financial or legal weight reviewed by a qualified lawyer before signing.

Will the resume pass applicant tracking systems?

The builder uses a single-column, standard-heading layout with selectable text, which is what these systems parse most reliably. Avoid adding graphics or tables, and tailor the wording to each job posting.

Do the QR codes expire or stop working?

No. The QR code is a static image encoding your link or text directly, with no redirect service in between, so it will keep working indefinitely and there is no scan limit or tracking.

Can I use these documents commercially?

Yes. There is no watermark, no attribution requirement and no licence fee. Invoices, resumes, proposals and codes you generate are yours to use in your business.
