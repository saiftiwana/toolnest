How It Works

This printable business card maker tool builds your card on a canvas sized to real print dimensions, so what exports matches what you designed. Six templates control genuinely different layouts — not just different colors — from Classic's centered symmetry to Modern's colored side panel to Creative's diagonal color split, and the QR code on the back reuses the same QR generation this site's dedicated QR Code Generator uses, so it scans reliably.

Key Features

As a business card maker with logo tool, upload your own logo and an optional headshot photo, then choose independent primary, accent, and text colors plus a font that applies consistently across the card. The back side supports four content types: a QR code (linking to your website, a vCard contact card, or a phone number), a short thank-you message, social links, or an address-focused layout. Export as PNG or JPG for digital sharing, or as a business card maker PDF combining both sides with optional bleed guides and crop marks for professional printing, and save your design as a draft to pick up again later in the same browser.

Common Use Cases

A freelancer building their first business card enters their details, tries a few templates against their brand colors, and adds a vCard QR code on the back so new contacts can save their info with one scan. A small business owner preparing cards for a trade show uses the Corporate template with their logo and a QR code linking to their website, then downloads the print-ready PDF with bleed guides for their printer. Someone updating an existing card's contact details reloads their saved draft, changes the phone number, and re-exports without rebuilding the whole design.

Tips

Keep text a reasonable distance from the card edges, especially if you plan to print with bleed, since content too close to the trim line risks being cut off — the bleed guide overlay in the PDF export shows exactly where the safe area sits. For the QR code on the back, the vCard option is especially useful at networking events since it lets people save your full contact details in one scan instead of just opening a link.

Frequently Asked Questions

What information should I include on a professional business card?

Name, job title, company name, and at least one or two ways to reach you (phone, email, or website) covers the essentials; a logo and tagline are common additions.

How do I create a print-ready business card file?

Choose your card size, design both sides, and download the PDF with bleed guides and crop marks enabled if your printer requires them.

Can I add a QR code to my business card design?

Yes, choose QR Code as the back side content and select whether it should link to your website, share your contact details as a vCard, or dial your phone number.

What file format should I download for printing?

PDF is best for professional printing since it combines both sides at the correct size; PNG or JPG work well for digital sharing or simple home printing.

Does the design include bleed and crop marks for printers?

Yes, enable the bleed guides and crop marks option before downloading the PDF if your printer needs them.

Can I save multiple business card versions and edit later?

Yes, use Save Draft to store your current design in this browser, and Load Draft to continue editing it later.

What information should I include on a professional business card?

Beyond name and contact details, a tagline or short description of what you do can help people remember your card after an event.

Is there a template option if I don't want to design from scratch?

Yes, all six templates come with a complete layout already applied — just fill in your details and colors.
