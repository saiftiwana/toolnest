Why Use This Tool

Calculating running pace and predicting race finish times online for free helps runners plan training and race strategy without a dedicated running app. This tool uses the Riegel formula to predict your finish time across standard race distances based on one known result. It removes manual math from race planning, giving instant predicted splits across all standard distances at once.

How It Works

This race-time-predictor applies the Riegel formula, a widely used exponential fatigue-factor model in running science, to extrapolate your predicted finish time at other distances from a single known distance and time input. It's a free, fast browser alternative to Runner's World pace calculator for quick race planning.

Key Features

The marathon-pace-estimator instantly predicts times for 5K, 10K, Half Marathon, and Full Marathon distances from a single input, saving you from manually calculating each distance separately. As a running-speed-tool, predictions update the moment you enter a valid distance and time, giving instant splits for race planning. All four standard race distances calculate simultaneously from a single time and distance input, saving repeated manual entry. Quick preset buttons fill in the standard 5K, 10K, Half Marathon, and Marathon distances, results now include pace per kilometer, pace per mile, and speed alongside the race predictions, and you can copy the full result or reset the tool in one click.

Common Use Cases

Runners training for an upcoming race predict their likely finish time based on a recent training run result. Coaches help athletes set realistic race pace goals using data-backed extrapolation rather than guesswork. Running clubs plan group pace targets for an upcoming event, and beginners set a first race goal time based on a shorter test run. Running groups compare predicted times across members to organize pace groups for a shared training run.

Tips for Best Results

The Riegel formula assumes reasonably consistent training and fitness across distances — actual race-day performance can vary based on terrain, weather, and race-day conditions beyond what the formula accounts for. Use predictions from a recent, well-executed training run for the most realistic extrapolation rather than an old or poorly-paced result. Retest predictions periodically as fitness improves, since an outdated baseline result will produce less accurate future predictions.

A Quick Note

Having a data-backed target time removes much of the guesswork from race day pacing, helping runners avoid starting too fast and fading later in the distance.
