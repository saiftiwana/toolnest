Why Use This Calculator

This calculator estimates your due date and current gestational age using Naegele's rule, entirely in your browser.

How It Works

This due-date-estimator applies Naegele's rule, a standard obstetric formula that adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of the last menstrual period, calculating both the estimated delivery date and current gestational age in weeks and days. It's a free, private client-side alternative to WhatToExpect and BabyCenter's due date calculators, since no personal health data is transmitted anywhere.

Key Features

The EDD-calculator and pregnancy-trimester-timeline tool shows your estimated delivery date alongside your current gestational week count, calculated instantly from the last period date you enter. Because it processes everything locally in your browser, this sensitive personal health information is never stored or sent to a server, unlike many pregnancy tracking apps that require an account. The current gestational age display updates automatically based on today date, so the same saved calculation stays useful across the following weeks. The result now also lists estimated 1st and 2nd trimester milestone dates alongside the due date, and a Copy Result button makes it easy to save the full summary for a prenatal appointment.

Common Use Cases

Individuals in early pregnancy get an initial due date estimate before their first prenatal appointment. Partners and family members calculate an estimated timeline to plan around an expected arrival. Healthcare students learn how Naegele's rule is applied as a standard obstetric estimation method, and anyone wanting a private estimate avoids apps requiring signup with sensitive pregnancy information. Employers and expecting parents plan leave scheduling around an estimated timeline well ahead of the actual delivery date.

Tips for Best Results

Naegele's rule assumes a standard 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14; if your cycle differs significantly from this average, your actual due date may vary from this estimate. A healthcare provider's ultrasound-based estimate, once available, is generally considered more precise than this last-period-based calculation and should take priority.

A Quick Note

Pregnancy-related information is deeply personal, so keeping this estimate entirely on-device offers meaningful peace of mind compared to signing up for an app that stores this data on a remote server indefinitely.
