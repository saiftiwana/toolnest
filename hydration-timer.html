Hydration Timer

Estimate daily water intake target from body weight — general wellness estimate.

Getting a Real Number Instead of "Drink More Water"

General advice to "drink more water" isn't actually a number you can plan around — this tool takes your body weight (and optionally activity level) and calculates a daily water intake target grounded in a widely used estimation formula, giving you an actual figure to aim for rather than a vague reminder.

The Formula Behind the Estimate

The calculation is based on a common general wellness guideline of roughly 30-35ml of water per kilogram of body weight per day as a starting baseline, adjusted upward for higher activity levels or hot climates, since both increase fluid loss through sweat beyond the baseline resting estimate. This is a widely cited general estimation approach, not a personalized medical calculation accounting for individual health conditions, medications, or specific climate and exertion details beyond the general inputs the tool asks for.

A Worked Example

A 70kg (roughly 154lb) person with a sedentary lifestyle gets a baseline estimate around 2.1-2.45 liters per day using the 30-35ml/kg range. The same person, if they exercise moderately most days or live somewhere consistently hot, would see that estimate adjusted upward to account for the additional fluid loss those factors typically cause — illustrating why a single generic "8 glasses a day" rule doesn't actually fit everyone's genuinely different needs based on body size and activity level.

Where a Specific Number Actually Helps

Someone starting a new fitness routine who wants a concrete daily hydration target rather than guessing. A person trying to build a more consistent hydration habit, using the calculated number to set a specific, trackable goal instead of an ambiguous "drink enough" reminder. Someone in a notably hot or high-altitude environment wanting a starting point adjusted for those conditions. Anyone curious how their casual daily water intake compares to a reasonable general estimate for their body size.

What This Estimate Doesn't Account For

Individual medical conditions (kidney issues, certain heart conditions, and several medications) can significantly change appropriate fluid intake in ways a general wellness calculator has no way to know about or account for — this is a general population estimate, not personalized medical guidance, and anyone with a relevant health condition should follow their doctor's specific recommendation over any general calculator's output.

Using the Timer Alongside the Calculation

Knowing the daily target number is only half the practical challenge — spreading that intake evenly across waking hours, rather than front-loading it all in the morning or scrambling to catch up in the evening, is generally considered a more comfortable and physiologically sensible approach; the built-in timer reminders help translate the daily total into a realistic, spaced-out drinking pattern rather than one big number to hit however you can.

Calculated Entirely On Your Device

The estimate is a straightforward arithmetic calculation run with client-side JavaScript — no health data you enter is transmitted anywhere, which matters for something as personal as body weight and daily habit information.

A Second Example

Someone who's been forgetting to drink water consistently through a busy workday enters their weight, gets a calculated 2.3-liter daily target, and sets timer reminders spaced every two hours during waking hours — turning an abstract daily number into a concrete, actionable schedule rather than an easily-forgotten mental note.

Is this calculation medically precise for my specific situation?

No — it's a general wellness estimate based on a widely used formula, not personalized medical guidance; anyone with a relevant health condition should follow their doctor's specific recommendation instead.

Does drinking coffee or tea count toward this total?

General guidance often counts most fluids, including caffeinated beverages in moderation, toward overall daily intake, though water itself remains the most straightforwardly counted source.

Why does my estimate change when I select a higher activity level?

Higher activity typically increases fluid loss through sweat, so the calculation adjusts the baseline estimate upward to account for that additional loss beyond resting needs.

Is more water always better than hitting the exact calculated number?

Not necessarily — significantly overdrinking water can, in rare cases, cause its own health issues; the calculated estimate is meant as a reasonable general target, not a floor to exceed as much as possible.

Should I use this if I have a kidney or heart condition?

Follow your doctor's specific fluid intake guidance instead, since certain medical conditions require individualized fluid management that a general wellness calculator isn't designed to account for.

Adjusting the Target Over Time

A calculated target based on current body weight and activity level is a reasonable starting point, but genuinely worth recalculating periodically — significant weight change, a shift in exercise routine, or moving to a different climate all meaningfully change the underlying inputs the formula depends on, so revisiting the estimate every few months (or after any major lifestyle change) keeps the target reasonably current rather than working from stale assumptions.
