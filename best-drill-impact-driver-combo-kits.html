Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Five things decide whether a drill impact driver combo kit fits your life. Ignore the marketing numbers; check these.

01

Two tools, two jobs

A drill/driver drills holes and drives screws gently, with a clutch that stops before you strip or snap them. An impact driver trades finesse for force — short bursts of rotational hammering that sink long screws and lag bolts without wrenching your wrist. A combo kit gives you both, which is why it's the standard first purchase.

02

Brushed vs. brushless

Brushed motors (DeWalt's DCD771/DCF885, the BLACK+DECKER) are proven and cheap, with brushes that eventually wear. Brushless motors (the DCD806B, both SKIL tools) are more efficient, run longer per charge, and pack more power into smaller bodies. Worth paying for if you'll use the tools for years; not a deal-breaker for occasional home use.

03

What's in the box

Count the batteries. Two batteries (DCK240C2, DCD771C2) means zero downtime. One battery (SKIL, BLACK+DECKER) means swapping mid-job. And "tool only" means no battery and no charger at all — a bargain only if you already own the platform's batteries. Also note: most kits include no drill or driver bits, so budget for a bit set.

04

Hammer mode

Regular drills cannot drill brick, block or concrete — they just spin and dull the bit. A hammer drill adds thousands of tiny blows per minute (the DCD806B: 34,000 BPM) that chip masonry as it spins. If your home has brick or concrete and you hang things on it, you need hammer mode at least in one tool you own.

05

The battery platform

You're not just buying tools; you're marrying a battery family. DeWalt 20V MAX is the biggest ecosystem here, SKIL PWRCore 20 is the budget riser, and BLACK+DECKER POWERCONNECT leans home-and-garden. Pick the platform by the tools you'll want next — every future bare tool gets cheaper once the batteries are on your shelf.

Frequently Asked Questions

DeWalt DCK240C2 vs DCD771C2 — which should I buy?

The DCD771C2 is the DCK240C2's drill (the DCD771) sold alone with the same two batteries, charger and bag. Buy the DCD771C2 if you only drill holes and drive ordinary screws. Buy the DCK240C2 combo if there's any chance of deck screws, lag bolts, fence work or long fastening runs in your future — the DCF885 impact driver does that work far faster and without twisting your wrist. During Black Friday the price gap often shrinks, which makes the combo the default choice.

Do I need a hammer drill, or is a regular drill/driver enough?

A regular drill/driver handles wood, metal, plastic and drywall — most home jobs. You need hammer mode only for masonry: brick, block, stone and concrete, where a normal drill just polishes the surface and ruins bits. If your walls are brick or concrete and you plan to anchor into them, add a hammer drill like the DCD806B; if not, a combo kit covers you.

Is brushless worth the extra money?

Brushless motors are more efficient (longer runtime per charge), more powerful for their size, and have no brushes to wear out. For a first kit on a budget, brushed tools like the DCK240C2's are proven and significantly cheaper — most homeowners will never wear one out. If you're buying for years of regular use, or you find a brushless combo on deal (the SKIL, or the DCD806B for masonry), it's the better long-term buy.

What does "tool only" actually mean?

It means the box contains the bare tool — no battery, no charger, usually just the tool and small accessories like a belt hook. Tool-only pricing looks like a steal (the bare DCD806B, for example) until you add a battery and charger. It's a genuine bargain only when you already own batteries from the same platform.

Right now, honestly: combo kits are the headline category of Black Friday tool sales. Do these kits include drill and driver bits?

Mostly, no. The DCK240C2 and DCD771C2 ship with tools, batteries, charger and bag — no bit sets. The SKIL kit includes two driver bits; the BLACK+DECKER 34-piece kit is the exception, bundling bits and basic hand tools. Whatever you buy, a decent impact-rated bit set is the first accessory to add.

Can I mix batteries between these brands?

No. DeWalt 20V MAX, SKIL PWRCore 20 and BLACK+DECKER POWERCONNECT batteries are not interchangeable across brands. Within a brand's platform, though, batteries swap freely between tools — which is exactly why your first kit choice matters: it sets the platform for every tool you add later.
