Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Six things decide whether a stud finder fits your walls and your work. Ignore the box art; check these.

01

Magnetic vs electronic

Magnetic finders (the C.H. Hanson) locate the screws and nails already holding your drywall — no batteries, no calibration, nothing to break, but silent wherever there is no fastener. Electronic finders sense the stud itself and work anywhere on the wall. Most people end up with an electronic as the main tool; the magnet is the backup that never dies.

02

How stud finders work

Electronic finders measure how the wall’s density changes as you slide — solid stud behind drywall reads differently from hollow wall. Edge-finders (the Zircon) report the stud’s two edges; multi-sensor arrays (both Franklins) read a whole strip of wall at once and display the stud’s full width, center included. More sensors means a clearer picture and fewer false alarms.

03

Drywall vs plaster & lath

Modern drywall is thin and uniform — every finder here handles it. Plaster and lath, standard in older US homes, is thicker and uneven, and it defeats shallow scanners. Reach for depth: the M210 scans to 1.7 in., the Zircon’s DeepScan to 1-1/2 in. Scan slowly, come from both directions, mark two studs instead of trusting one, and confirm with a tiny test hole somewhere it won’t show.

04

Live wires: warnings, not guarantees

Three of these five watch for live AC: the M210’s Live Wire Meter, the Zircon’s WireWarning, and the Bosch’s dedicated live-wire mode (to 50 mm). All three are warning layers, not X-ray vision — a wire can be missed, shielded or dead-looking and live. Whatever your finder says, switch the breaker off before you drill into a wall. Every time.

05

Edge-finding vs full-width

Anchors and lag bolts belong in the stud’s center, and the two styles get you there differently. An edge-finder makes you find both edges and split the difference — cheap and proven, but two chances to mis-mark. A full-width display shows center and edges in one pass, which is why the Franklins anchor this page: for a TV mount, center is the whole game.

06

Hanging heavy things

For TVs, cabinets and loaded shelves: find the stud’s center, then verify with a second pass and a second stud — studs usually run 16 in. apart, and a spacing that suddenly isn’t 16 in. means you found something else. Drill a small pilot hole to confirm wood before the big bit, use the mount maker’s lag bolts into the center of the stud, and never trust drywall anchors alone for a TV.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best stud finder for most homeowners?

The Franklin Sensors ProSensor M210. Its 13 sensors light up the full width of the stud — center and both edges — in a single pass, it scans up to 1.7 in. deep, it never needs calibration, and its Live Wire Meter warns you about live AC behind the wall. For mounting TVs and shelves, that combination is hard to beat.

Magnetic or electronic stud finder — which is better?

They find different things. A magnetic finder like the C.H. Hanson 03040 finds the screws and nails holding the drywall: it is dead simple and never needs batteries, but it only works where a fastener is. An electronic finder senses the stud itself, works anywhere on the wall, and the better ones warn about live wires. Most homeowners end up happier with electronic; magnetic is the unbeatable drawer backup.

Can a stud finder find studs behind plaster and lath?

Yes, but plaster is the hardest common wall: it is thicker and less uniform than drywall. Deep-scanning models help — the Franklin M210 scans up to 1.7 in. deep and the Zircon StudSensor e50 has a DeepScan mode rated to 1-1/2 in. Go slowly, scan from both directions, mark more than one stud, and confirm with a tiny test hole somewhere inconspicuous before you commit to big anchors.

Do stud finders detect live electrical wires?

Some warn you, and the wording matters: the Franklin M210 has a Live Wire Meter, the Zircon e50 has WireWarning for live unshielded AC, and the Bosch GMS120-27 has a dedicated live-wire detection depth of 50 mm. Treat every one of these as a backup warning, never a guarantee — switch the breaker off before you drill into any wall.

Why does my stud finder give false readings?

On an edge-finder like the Zircon e50, the usual causes are calibrating while already over a stud, sliding too fast, or tilting the unit off the wall mid-scan. Calibrate on a blank stretch of wall, keep the finder flat, and slide slowly. Multi-sensor finders like the two Franklins skip calibration entirely, which removes the most common cause of bad readings.
