Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Five things decide whether a tape measure fits your work. Ignore the box art; check these.

01

Standout & reach

Standout is how far the blade extends before it buckles. Solo measuring across rooms and ceilings lives or dies on it: the FATMAX here lists 14 ft of reach, the DEWALT Atomic Compact 13 ft. More reach means fewer trips back down the ladder.

02

Blade width

Wider blades stand out farther and carry bigger, easier-to-read numbers — the trade is a bigger case. Narrower blades, like the PowerLock’s 1-inch blade, make a slimmer tape that pockets easily. Pick which side of that trade your work sits on.

03

The lock

The lock is the control you touch on every measurement. Slide locks are the classic (PowerLock), a finger brake stops the blade precisely (DEWALT), and a self-locking blade stays out until you press the button (Komelon). There’s no best — only the one your hand prefers.

04

Magnetic tips

A magnetic hook grabs steel studs, conduit and ductwork and turns two-person measurements into solo work — that’s the Milwaukee’s whole case. The same magnet collects screws and metal filings, so wipe the hook now and then.

05

Markings

Big numbers, stud-center marks and fractional markings decide how fast you read the blade — and how rarely you misread it. DEWALT prints fractionals on both sides of the blade; the FATMAX pairs large numbers with standard stud markers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best tape measure for most people?

A 25 ft tape with a wide blade and long standout. Our top pick is the Stanley FATMAX 33-725: 14 ft of reach, a coated blade with BladeArmor at the hook, and a case built for daily use. If you measure alone often, reach matters more than any other spec on the box.

What does standout (max reach) mean on a tape measure?

It is how far the blade can extend on its own before it buckles and falls. Manufacturers list it as max reach: 14 ft on the FATMAX in this guide, 13 ft on the DEWALT Atomic Compact. Longer reach means easier solo measuring across rooms and ceilings.

Is a magnetic tape measure worth it?

If you work around steel — studs, conduit, ductwork, appliances — yes: the magnetic hook, like the one on the Milwaukee 48-22-0325, holds the end of the blade for you and turns a two-person measure into a one-person job. For wood-only DIY it is a nice extra, not a necessity.

25 ft or 35 ft — which length should I buy?

25 ft covers rooms, lumber and furniture in almost every home, and the case stays smaller and cheaper. Go 35 ft only if you regularly measure across big yards, roofs or long framing runs. All five tapes in this guide are 25 ft on purpose.

What is a self-locking tape measure?

The blade stays extended on its own as you pull it out — no lock to hold down — and retracts when you press a button. The Komelon SL2825 works this way, and it is the easiest style for quick, one-handed measuring around the house.
