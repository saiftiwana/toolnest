Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Five specs decide whether an electric screwdriver fits your life. Ignore the marketing numbers; check these.

01

Voltage

Home drivers are 3.6V–4V, and that's enough. Voltage here isn't about power bragging — every pick on this page handles household screws. Go higher only if you're actually shopping for a drill-driver.

02

Torque

The number that matters most. Furniture and household work wants roughly 3–5 N·m (about 22–44 in-lbs). Precision electronics want the opposite: under 0.2 N·m, so tiny screws aren't snapped. Adjustable settings beat one fixed speed.

03

USB-C charging

Every pick here charges over USB, most over USB-C. That means no proprietary dock to lose and you can top up from a laptop or power bank. The DeWalt refills in about 60 minutes; smaller batteries trade capacity for size.

04

Bit selection

A driver without the right bit is a paperweight. For homes, 12–25 standard 1/4″ hex bits cover nearly everything (HOTO's 25, SKIL's 28). For electronics you need a dedicated precision set — Fanttik's 50 magnetic bits.

05

Weight & grip

You'll hold this tool overhead, inside cabinets, at arm's length. Under a pound (DeWalt: 0.73 lbs) feels effortless; around 16 oz (SKIL) is still comfortable. A pivoting head beats raw power when screws hide in corners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are electric screwdrivers strong enough for furniture?

Yes — that's their home turf. Flat-pack and ready-to-assemble furniture uses small screws in soft materials, well inside the 22–44 in-lbs these drivers deliver. Where they struggle is large screws into hardwood or studs; that's drill territory. Use the low torque setting on cam locks and particleboard so you don't over-tighten and crack the panel.

How much torque do I actually need?

For household work, about 3–5 N·m (22–44 in-lbs) covers furniture, hinges and fixtures. For electronics, you need far less — 0.05–0.2 N·m — because tiny screws strip or snap under household-driver torque. More torque is not better; the right, adjustable range for your jobs is.

Is USB-C charging important?

More than you'd think. USB-C means one cable charges your phone and your screwdriver, there's no proprietary dock to lose in a move, and you can recharge from a laptop or power bank. Every pick on this page charges over USB — treat a model that needs a special cradle as a mark against it.

Can an electric screwdriver replace a drill?

No — and it isn't trying to. A screwdriver can't drill holes, drive lag screws, or handle masonry and studs. What it does better than a drill is everything small: it's lighter, slower, stops before stripping, and fits where a drill never will. Most homes end up owning both, and reaching for the screwdriver 90% of the time.

How long does the battery last?

It depends on capacity. The DeWalt's 2Ah battery is rated for up to 680 screws per charge; the Fanttik precision driver claims 400+ small screws per charge from its tiny 350mAh cell. In practice, any pick here finishes a full furniture build on one charge. The catch: most batteries are built in and not replaceable, so expect the tool's life to match the battery's.

What's the difference between electric and manual torque?

Electric torque is what the motor delivers before it stops — that's your working limit (4 N·m on the HOTO). Manual torque is how much force you can add by twisting the tool by hand with the motor off (8 N·m on the HOTO, 124 in-lbs on the DeWalt). Manual torque is for the final snug turn, or breaking a stubborn screw loose — it's a backup, not the spec to buy on.
