How It Works

This minimalist logo maker tool represents your logo as an icon and typography combination, letting you switch between 9 distinct styles that change composition and typography together — not just color — plus 5 layout arrangements and 14 simple geometric icons. Because the icon shapes and the exported SVG are generated from the exact same underlying vector path data, what you see in the live preview is exactly what exports, whether that's a scalable SVG file or a raster PNG or JPG.

Key Features

As a business logo maker online tool, colors apply through Primary, Secondary, and Accent fields plus six curated palettes (Monochrome, Dark, Light, Corporate, Creative, Luxury) that set all three at once. Typography controls cover font family, weight, size, letter spacing, and uppercase/lowercase, and the icon can be resized, rotated, and spaced independently from the text. Common Use Cases

A startup founder without a design budget builds a first logo using the Bold style and their brand colors, checks how it looks stacked for a square social profile picture versus horizontal for a website header, then exports both. Someone rebranding an existing small business tries several of the 9 styles with the same brand name to compare a Minimal wordmark against a Geometric icon-forward version before choosing a direction. A freelancer preparing brand assets for a client exports the SVG for their website, a transparent PNG for use over photos, and the Black/White variations for single-color printing contexts like an invoice letterhead.

Tips

An SVG export is the most flexible format to keep as your primary logo file, since it scales to any size — from a favicon to a banner — without losing quality, while PNG with a transparent background works best for placing your logo over photos or colored backgrounds. If your brand name is long, the Stacked or Text Only layouts often read more clearly at small sizes than a wide horizontal Icon + Text arrangement, which can start to feel cramped once scaled down.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I create a professional logo for free without hiring a designer?

Yes, choosing a style, icon, and color palette produces a complete, usable logo with no design experience required.

What logo file formats should I download for different use cases?

SVG is best for anything requiring scalability (like large printing or a website header), transparent PNG works well for overlaying on photos or backgrounds, and JPG suits places that don't accept transparency.

How do I ensure my logo looks good on business cards and websites?

Check the Full Color, Black, and White variations in the gallery, since a logo that needs to print in a single color or appear on a dark background should still read clearly.

Can I edit my logo after downloading and make changes?

The downloaded files are final exports; to make changes, return to this tool, use Load Draft if you saved one, and re-export after adjusting.

Does the logo maker provide copyright and commercial use rights?

The icons are simple generic geometric shapes (not trademarked symbols), and the design you create by combining them with your own text and colors is yours to use commercially.

What's the difference between raster (PNG) and vector (SVG) logo files?

A vector SVG file scales to any size without losing sharpness, while a raster PNG is a fixed-resolution image that can look blurry or pixelated if enlarged significantly beyond its original size.

Can I use my brand colors in the logo maker?

Yes, set Primary, Secondary, and Accent colors individually using the color pickers to match your exact brand palette.

Can I download my logo in high resolution for print?

Yes, PNG and JPG exports render at a high fixed resolution suitable for most print uses, and SVG scales to any size without quality loss.
