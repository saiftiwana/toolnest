Common Use Cases

Small retail shop owners create barcodes for products that didn't come with manufacturer barcodes. Inventory managers generate internal tracking codes for warehouse organization. Craft and handmade goods sellers create scannable price tags for market stalls or pop-up shops, and event organizers generate barcodes for simple ticket or check-in systems. Library and rental businesses generate internal barcodes for tracking loaned items through a simple check-in and check-out system. Home-based businesses assign consistent tracking codes to handmade batches for quality and stock record purposes.

Tips for Best Results

CODE128 barcodes work with any combination of numbers and most standard characters, making them flexible for internal tracking systems that don't need to match a specific retail barcode standard like UPC or EAN. Test scan a printed barcode with a phone scanning app before a full print run to confirm it reads correctly. A high-contrast printout, black lines on a white background, scans far more reliably than a low-contrast or colored variant.

A Quick Note

Reliable, scannable codes for inventory or contact information help small operations stay organized without the overhead of enterprise-level tracking software.

CODE128 is a widely supported barcode standard that can encode letters and numbers, commonly used in shipping, inventory, and retail labeling.
