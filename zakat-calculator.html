Why Sunni and Shia Zakat Calculations Are Different

Zakat calculators often assume one set of rules fits everyone, but Sunni (Hanafi) and Shia (Jafari) fiqh genuinely differ in scope, not just in rate. In Hanafi fiqh, Zakat is due on gold, silver, cash, bank savings, and business assets at 2.5%, using a nisab of 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver. In Jafari fiqh as commonly explained per Grand Ayatollah Sistani's rulings, Zakat is obligatory on nine specific categories — gold and silver only in coin form, four grains (wheat, barley, dates, raisins), and livestock — using separate, lower nisab thresholds (69.12 grams for gold, 483.88 grams for silver). Ordinary cash and bank savings are not directly zakatable in Shia fiqh; instead, they are purified through Khums, a distinct 20% obligation on annual net savings. A calculator built for Pakistani and Indian Muslims following either fiqh needs to reflect this properly rather than forcing both traditions into the same categories.

What is the difference between Sunni and Shia Zakat calculation?

Sunni (Hanafi) Zakat applies broadly to gold, silver, cash, and business assets at 2.5%. Shia (Jafari) Zakat applies only to specific categories — gold/silver coins, four grains, and livestock — with cash and savings instead purified through a separate 20% Khums obligation.

What is the Zakat Nisab for gold and silver in grams?

In Sunni (Hanafi) fiqh, nisab is 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver. In Shia (Jafari) fiqh per Sistani rulings, nisab is lower — 69.12 grams for gold coins and 483.88 grams for silver coins.

Do I pay Zakat on gold jewelry I wear regularly?

In Hanafi fiqh, most scholars hold that Zakat is due on all gold and silver you own, including worn jewelry. In Shia fiqh, jewelry is generally not zakatable unless it is in coin form used as currency.

What is Khums and how is it different from Zakat?

Khums is a distinct Shia obligation of 20% on the surplus remaining from your annual income after necessary expenses, calculated on your personal Khums due date — it is separate from, and in addition to, any Zakat that may be due.

How is Zakat calculated on agricultural produce (Ushr)?

Ushr is 10% of the harvest if the land was watered by rain, rivers, or natural moisture, or 5% if it was irrigated using paid means like a tube well or motor — and in Hanafi fiqh there is no nisab threshold for Ushr.

Does Zakat apply to fruit that spoils and can't be stored?

Perishable fruit that can't be stored is typically sold quickly; the cash received from that sale is then included in your regular Zakat calculation on cash and savings, rather than being taxed at a fixed Ushr rate.
