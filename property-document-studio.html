Property Paperwork Guide

What is a Rent Agreement?

A rent agreement (or rental/lease agreement) is a written contract between a landlord and tenant setting out the rent, deposit, tenancy period, and responsibilities of each party.

What should a Rent Agreement contain?

At minimum: full details of both parties, the property description, monthly rent and deposit, tenancy start/end dates, and each party's responsibilities for maintenance and utilities.

What documents are commonly required for renting?

Commonly requested items include identity documents for both parties, proof of address, and sometimes proof of income — exact requirements vary locally.

What should buyers check before purchasing property?

Ownership/title documents, the seller's identity, any outstanding dues or encumbrances, and the accuracy of the property's description and measurements are commonly checked — verify the full list with a local property lawyer.

What is a Sale Deed?

A Sale Deed is the legal document that formally transfers ownership of property from a seller to a buyer, typically registered with the relevant local authority.

What documents should be reviewed before signing?

Names, identity details, the property address and description, all dates and amounts, and the presence of required signatures and witnesses are commonly reviewed before signing any property document.

What is Stamp Duty?

Stamp duty is a government tax charged on certain property transactions and documents, calculated as a percentage of the property or transaction value — rates vary significantly by jurisdiction.

What is Registration Fee?

A registration fee is a charge for officially recording a property transaction with the relevant land or property registration authority, separate from stamp duty.

How is Property Tax generally calculated?

Property tax is commonly calculated as a percentage of either the assessed property value or annual rental value, with the exact method and rate set by the local municipal or tax authority.

What is Security Deposit?

A security deposit is an upfront amount, often a multiple of the monthly rent, held by the landlord to cover potential damages or unpaid rent, and is typically refundable at the end of the tenancy.

What should landlords and tenants check?

Landlords commonly verify tenant identity and agree responsibilities clearly in writing; tenants commonly verify the landlord's ownership and get all promises documented in the agreement itself, not just verbally.

What should be verified locally?

Exact document requirements, stamp duty rates, registration fees, and property tax rates should always be verified with the relevant local authority or a qualified professional, since they vary by province, state, city, and property type.
