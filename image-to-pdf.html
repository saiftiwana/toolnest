    Turning a Folder of Photos Into One PDF

    Emailing five separate JPGs to someone almost always ends with at least one arriving out of order, or the recipient asking "can you just send this as one file?" This tool stacks your JPG or PNG images into a single PDF, in the order you choose, built entirely inside your browser tab.

    How the Pages Get Built

    Each image you add is drawn onto its own PDF page at a size matching its aspect ratio, using an in-browser PDF-generation library — no server processes your photos at any point. Because this runs as client-side JavaScript, a 10-photo batch assembles in roughly the same time it takes to load the images themselves, without a queue or upload progress bar standing between you and the download.

    A Worked Example

    Say you're submitting six photos of a damaged shipment for an insurance claim — each shot at a different phone resolution, some portrait, some landscape. Uploaded in sequence, each photo becomes its own page sized to match that photo's orientation, producing one 6-page PDF you can attach to a single email instead of six image files the claims adjuster has to open one by one.

    Common Situations This Solves

    Someone applying for a visa combines scanned passport pages and a photo ID into one document, since most application portals accept only a single PDF upload, not separate images. A contractor documents a job site with several progress photos assembled into one dated report. A student photographs handwritten homework pages and needs them as one file for an upload portal that rejects multiple images. In each case, the underlying problem is the same — a portal or recipient wants one file, and all you have is several photos.

    Reordering and Page Setup

    Photos convert in the order you add them, and most versions of this tool let you drag to reorder before generating the final PDF, so you don't need to re-select files from scratch if the sequence needs adjusting. If your images are a mix of portrait and landscape, each page keeps its own source orientation rather than forcing everything into a single fixed page shape, which avoids the awkward white borders you'd get from cramming a portrait photo onto a landscape page.

    What This Tool Isn't

    This converts images to PDF pages as-is; it doesn't compress, crop, or straighten the photos first. If your images are large phone camera files, expect a correspondingly larger PDF — run them through the Image Compressor first if file size matters more than resolution. It also isn't a document scanner: photos of paper documents come through as photos, not perspective-corrected scans (use the Document Scanner & PDF Maker for that).

    Nothing Uploaded, Nothing Stored

    Every image is read and assembled with client-side JavaScript directly in your browser — none of your photos, including sensitive ones like ID documents or insurance evidence, are transmitted to a server during conversion.

    Can I mix JPG and PNG images in the same PDF?

    Yes — the tool accepts both formats in any combination, converting each to its own page regardless of the original image type.

    Does converting to PDF reduce my image quality?

    No meaningful quality loss occurs during the page-embedding step itself, though the final PDF's file size will reflect your original images' resolution and compression.

    Can I reorder photos after uploading them?

    Yes — drag to rearrange the thumbnail order before generating the PDF, since final page order follows whatever sequence is shown at export time.

    Will a portrait photo and a landscape photo look right on the same page size?

    Each page is sized to match its own source image's orientation rather than forced onto one fixed page shape, so portrait and landscape photos both display without distortion or excess white space.

    Is there a limit on how many images I can combine?

    There's no fixed cap, though a very large batch of high-resolution photos will take proportionally longer since everything processes on your device.

    Comparing This to a Phone's Built-In "Print to PDF"

    Many phones can already save a photo as PDF through the share/print menu, but that path usually handles one image at a time and often adds unwanted margins or scaling. Combining several images into a single ordered PDF in one pass, with each page sized to its own photo, is the specific gap this tool fills that a single-image print dialog doesn't cover.

    A Second Example

    A property manager documenting move-out condition takes twelve photos across different rooms, then combines them into one PDF report attached to the tenant's file — twelve separate image attachments in an email thread get lost or reordered over time, while one dated PDF stays intact as a single reference document years later.
