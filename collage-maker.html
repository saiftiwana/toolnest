    Turning Several Photos Into One Shareable Image

    Posting four separate vacation photos to a story means four separate taps for anyone viewing them — and most social platforms compress each upload anyway. Combining them into a single collage grid solves both problems: one clean image, arranged the way you want, built entirely in your browser.

    How the Grid Is Built

    Each photo you add is drawn onto its own cell of an HTML5 canvas sized to your chosen layout — a 2-photo side-by-side split, a 3-photo mixed grid, or a 4-photo even quad. The tool automatically crops each source image to fill its cell without stretching or distorting the aspect ratio, then flattens everything into one exportable image once you're happy with the arrangement.

    A Real Layout Example

    Say you have four photos from a weekend trip at different resolutions — a 4032×3024 phone shot, a 1920×1080 screenshot, and two more phone photos. Dropped into the 4-photo grid layout, each is auto-cropped to a matching square cell so the final collage comes out as one uniform 2000×2000px image regardless of how mismatched the originals were, ready to post without any manual cropping beforehand.

    Where People Use This

    Travelers summarizing a trip into one Instagram-ready post instead of a multi-photo carousel. Parents combining several photos from a birthday party into a single image to text to family. Small businesses building a simple before/after or multi-angle product image without design software. Anyone making a simple "then vs now" 2-photo comparison for social media.

    Choosing the Right Grid

    Two photos work best for direct comparisons — before/after, side-by-side outfits, two angles of the same product. Three photos suit an uneven story (one highlight photo plus two supporting ones). Four photos is the most balanced option for a general trip or event recap where no single photo needs to dominate. Matching the layout to the story you're telling matters more than cramming in extra photos.

    Where It's Not the Right Tool

    This is a fixed-grid collage tool, not a free-form scrapbook layout — it won't let you rotate individual photos at odd angles or add decorative borders and stickers. If you need more than 4 photos in one image, or a Pinterest-style irregular collage, a dedicated design tool will serve you better; this tool is built for the fast, clean 2-4 photo case.

    Handled Fully On-Device

    Every photo you add is read and composited using client-side JavaScript and the Canvas API — none of your photos are uploaded anywhere during the process, so private trip or family photos stay on your device until you choose to export and share the finished collage yourself.

    Can I rearrange which photo goes in which cell?

    Yes — remove and re-add photos in the order you want them to appear, since each upload fills the next available cell in sequence.

    Why does part of my photo get cropped out in the collage?

    Each cell has a fixed aspect ratio, so a photo that doesn't match that ratio gets center-cropped to fill the cell cleanly rather than stretched, which would distort it.

    What's the maximum number of photos I can combine?

    The tool supports 2 to 4 photos per collage, which covers the most common social-media and messaging use cases without the layout becoming cluttered.

    Can I add spacing or a border between the photos?

    The grid layouts include a thin default spacing between cells; there's currently no adjustable border width control beyond the built-in gap.

    What resolution is the final collage image?

    The output resolution scales based on your uploaded photos' sizes, aiming for a uniform, high-quality result rather than a fixed small preview size.

    Matching Photo Orientation to Layout

    Mixing portrait and landscape photos in the same grid usually looks better than it sounds, since each cell auto-crops to fit — but for the cleanest result, pair two portrait phone photos in a 2-photo side-by-side layout rather than forcing a wide landscape screenshot into the same narrow cell, where auto-cropping would cut off more of the original frame than necessary.

    Keeping File Size Reasonable

    Combining four high-resolution phone photos (each 4-5MB) into one collage naturally produces a larger single file than any one source photo, since the canvas holds all four at full detail. If the resulting collage feels too large for a quick text message, run it through the Image Compressor afterward — a collage built from photos, not screenshots, usually tolerates 70-80% quality with no visible difference, shrinking the shareable file significantly before you send it.

    A Second Example Layout

    For a 2-photo before/after comparison — say, a room before and after cleaning — the side-by-side layout places both images at equal width with a thin divider, so the viewer's eye naturally compares left to right without needing captions. This is a different use case from the 4-photo trip recap: fewer photos, a specific comparison message, and a layout chosen deliberately to support that message rather than just fitting more content into one frame.
