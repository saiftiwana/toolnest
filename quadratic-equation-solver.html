How It Works

This quadratic-formula-solver calculates the discriminant (b² − 4ac) first, then applies the standard quadratic formula to compute either two distinct real roots, one repeated real root, or a pair of complex roots depending on whether the discriminant is positive, zero, or negative. It's a free, fast browser alternative to Symbolab and WolframAlpha's quadratic solvers for algebra homework.

The polynomial-root-finder automatically detects which of the three root scenarios applies based on the discriminant sign, displaying results in the appropriate format — decimal values for real roots, or a plus-minus imaginary expression for complex roots. How does the quadratic formula solve for variable roots?

The formula x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a calculates the roots directly from the equation's coefficients a, b, and c.

What does a negative discriminant mean when solving a quadratic equation?

A negative discriminant means the equation has no real roots, only a pair of complex conjugate roots, indicating the parabola doesn't cross the x-axis.

Can coefficient 'a' be zero in a quadratic equation?

No, if a equals zero the equation becomes linear rather than quadratic, so the tool requires a nonzero value for a.

What does it mean when the discriminant equals exactly zero?

A discriminant of zero means the equation has exactly one repeated real root, where the parabola touches the x-axis at a single point.
