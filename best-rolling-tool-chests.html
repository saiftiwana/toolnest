Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Six things decide whether a rolling chest fits your work. Ignore the headline capacity until you have checked these.

01

You are choosing a system, not a box

Four of these five boxes are the rolling base of a modular family — PACKOUT, ToughSystem, VERSASTACK, MODbox — and each family only stacks within itself. The base you buy today decides which drawers, organizers and crates you can add for the next decade. Already own boxes from one system? Buy its base. Starting fresh? Compare the accessory range you will actually want, not just the box in front of you.

02

Load ratings: box vs system

A single number hides two different claims. A box rating (Milwaukee and DEWALT: 250 lb) is what that box carries on its own. A system rating is what a whole stacked tower carries, with the load shared into the base. Klein states both for the MODbox: 250 lb for the stacked system, 70 lb across its two drawers. Before you compare two boxes, make sure you are comparing the same kind of number.

03

Wheels decide where it can go

Wheel diameter is destiny. The 7 in. wheels on the Keter are fine on concrete and garage floors. At 8–9 in. (DEWALT, Milwaukee) gravel, cords and rough ground stop being obstacles. The Klein's 10 in. solid rubber wheels are the largest here and the best choice for genuinely rough terrain. If your route crosses grass, gravel or site debris, buy wheels first and capacity second.

04

Weather sealing: IP65, IP54, or none

IP65 (Milwaukee, DEWALT) means dust-tight and sealed against water jets — truck-bed-in-the-rain protection. IP54 (CRAFTSMAN) stops most dust and splashing water; fine for garages and covered work. And some honest boxes carry no rating at all: Klein lists the MODbox drawer box as not weather resistant. Match the seal to where the box will actually live and travel, not to the best number on the page.

05

Access: tub, half lid, or drawers

An open tub swallows the biggest tools but buries the small ones, and reaching the bottom means unstacking. A half lid (CRAFTSMAN) lets you grab a tool without moving the stack, at the cost of a smaller opening. Drawers (Klein) are the most organized and the only true no-unstack access, but each drawer has its own weight limit and deep, bulky tools may not fit. Pick the access style that matches what you reach for most.

06

Size, weight and the ride home

A rolling chest has to fit your vehicle and your storage corner before it has to fit your tools. Check the footprint and the height with the handle extended (the Klein reaches 44 in.), and remember the box's own weight rides along: the Klein is 44.5 lb empty, the resin Keter system far lighter. If the chest lives in a van or gets lifted into a truck daily, empty weight is a working spec, not a footnote.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best rolling tool chest for most people?

For most garages and jobsites, the Milwaukee PACKOUT Rolling Tool Box. It carries 250 lb, rolls on 9 in. all-terrain wheels, seals out weather to IP65, and it is the base of the biggest modular system in the business — every box you add later clicks onto it. If you would rather not pay the PACKOUT premium, the DEWALT ToughSystem 2.0 matches it on capacity and sealing for rough work, and the CRAFTSMAN VERSASTACK is the budget way into a rolling chest.

Can I mix boxes from different brands, like PACKOUT with ToughSystem?

No — and this is the most important thing to understand before you buy. Each modular system only stacks and locks within itself: PACKOUT boxes fit PACKOUT, ToughSystem fits ToughSystem, VERSASTACK fits VERSASTACK, MODbox fits MODbox. The rolling box you buy first is the foundation of a system, so choose the system, not just the box. If you already own boxes from one system, the rolling base from that same system is almost always the right pick.

What do load capacity numbers actually mean?

Read them carefully, because brands count two different things. A box rating is what the box itself can carry. A system rating is what a whole stacked tower can carry, most of it sitting in the bottom box. The Klein MODbox on this page is the clearest example: Klein rates the drawers at 70 lb total (50 lb in the lower drawer, 20 lb in the top), while the 250 lb figure is the stacked system's rating. For a single rolling box used on its own, compare box ratings; the 250 lb figures on the Milwaukee and DEWALT are ratings for those boxes themselves.

Are drawers or an open tub better in a rolling tool box?

They solve different problems. An open tub (Milwaukee, DEWALT) swallows big tools whole — a circular saw, a rotary hammer — but everything rides in one pile unless you add trays or smaller boxes on top. Drawers (the Klein) keep tools separated and reachable without unstacking anything, but each drawer has its own weight limit and bulky tools may not fit. The CRAFTSMAN splits the difference with a half lid you can open without unstacking the boxes above it.

What does IP65 or IP54 weather sealing mean on a tool box?

The IP code rates protection against dust and water. IP65 means dust-tight and sealed against water jets — a box that can ride in an open truck bed through rain. IP54, like the CRAFTSMAN here, blocks most dust and splashing water but is not storm-proof. And some boxes carry no rating at all: Klein's own datasheet lists the MODbox drawer box as not weather resistant, so it belongs under a canopy, in a van or in the garage rather than out in the weather.
